# Arabic Syntax and Agreement Core

Use this reference for Arabic proofreading and correctness-heavy editing when the task requires discovering grammatical defects without being told the rule in advance.

This file is a compact operational synthesis, not a substitute for the cited grammars. Its purpose is to improve **detection + adjudication** while preserving Arab Writer's minimal-edit discipline.

## Governing principle

Before deciding that a suspicious form is wrong, identify:

1. the governing particle, verb, or construction;
2. the syntactic role of the target word or phrase;
3. whether the role is visible in letters/word form or only in optional case vowels;
4. whether classical or modern Arabic licenses more than one analysis;
5. the smallest correction that fixes a real defect.

Do not preserve an error merely because the sentence sounds fluent. Do not correct a licensed form merely because another form is more common.

## Discovery pass for blind proofreading

Scan the sentence for high-yield grammatical triggers before returning it unchanged:

- `إن` and sisters;
- `كان` and sisters;
- `لا` النافية للجنس;
- negation with `إلا`;
- passive verbs, especially verbs that take two objects;
- duals and sound plurals whose case is visible in `ان/ين` or `ون/ين`;
- idafa involving dual/sound masculine plural nun deletion;
- relative pronouns and their antecedents;
- human vs non-human plural agreement;
- adjective/apposition/coordination case agreement;
- five nouns;
- jussive/subjunctive environments whose effect is visible in the written form.

A proofreading pass should therefore be **diagnostic before conservative**: first test these relations, then preserve what survives the test.

## 1. Nominal clauses and fronting

A nominal clause may appear in the ordinary order `مبتدأ + خبر`, or with a fronted predicate, especially when the predicate is a prepositional phrase or adverbial phrase.

Examples:

- `في المكتبة كتابٌ نادرٌ.` — fronted predicate + delayed subject.
- `لدى الفريق خطتان واضحتان.` — fronted predicate + delayed subject.

Do not reorder a correct fronted structure merely because the ordinary order is more familiar.

## 2. `إن` and sisters

`إن` and its sisters normally:

- assign accusative to the noun;
- leave the predicate nominative.

The predicate may be fronted and the noun delayed.

Examples:

- `إن في التقرير ملاحظاتٍ مهمةً.`
- `إن لدى اللجنة خيارين متاحين.`

When the delayed noun is a dual or sound plural, check the **visible case form**, not only optional vowels.

Do not assume that the first noun-like expression after `إن` is its noun; fronted prepositional predicates are common.

## 3. `كان` and sisters

`كان` and its sisters normally:

- leave the noun nominative;
- assign accusative to the predicate.

The predicate may be fronted and the noun delayed.

Examples:

- `كان في المجلس أعضاءٌ جددٌ.`
- `كانت لدى الإدارة خططٌ بديلةٌ.`

A delayed noun after fronted predicate material remains nominative. Do not convert it to accusative merely because it comes late in the sentence.

## 4. `لا` النافية للجنس

When `لا` genuinely functions as `لا` النافية للجنس:

- its noun is governed by `لا`;
- if the noun is added (مضاف), it is accusative;
- do not confuse it with ordinary negative `لا` or prohibitive `لا`.

Examples:

- `لا طالبَ علمٍ مستغنٍ عن المراجعة.`
- `لا صاحبَ حقٍّ مضطرٌّ إلى ترك حجته.`

Determine the function of `لا` from context before editing.

## 5. Exception with `إلا`

Do not apply a blanket rule that the noun after `إلا` must always be accusative.

### Emptied exception (الاستثناء المفرغ)

After negation or similar restriction, when the clause has no explicit excluded class, the expression after `إلا` takes the syntactic role required by the verb or clause.

Examples:

- `ما حضر إلا طالبٌ.` — subject, nominative.
- `ما قابلتُ إلا طالبًا.` — object, accusative.
- `ما مررتُ إلا بطالبٍ.` — governed by the preposition.

Therefore identify the missing role first. A transitive verb with an explicit subject may still require an accusative object after `إلا`.

### Complete constructions

Complete affirmative/negative exception constructions have additional rules and recognized variants. If the supplied context genuinely supports more than one classical analysis, preserve or flag rather than forcing one mechanical pattern.

## 6. Passive voice and deputy subject

In the passive, the element promoted to نائب الفاعل is nominative.

With verbs that take two objects, the first object normally becomes deputy subject and the second remains in its object role.

Fresh example:

- Active: `منح المديرُ الباحثينَ صلاحيةً إضافيةً.`
- Passive: `مُنح الباحثون صلاحيةً إضافيةً.`

Do not preserve an accusative first object after a clearly passive ditransitive verb when that object is functioning as deputy subject.

## 7. Human and non-human plural agreement

### Human plural

Human plural nouns normally take full plural agreement where standard modern syntax requires it.

Example:

- `الباحثون ملتزمون بالمنهج.`

### Non-human plural

A non-human plural is normally treated as feminine singular in adjective, predicate, pronoun, and relative-reference agreement in standard Arabic.

Examples:

- `الكتب الجديدة مفيدة.`
- `القرارات التي صدرت أمس نافذة.`
- `البيانات متسقة مع التعريف.`

Do not replace a required feminine-singular agreement pattern with masculine singular or human plural agreement.

### Heritage and semantic agreement

Arabic also licenses agreement by meaning in some constructions, especially with:

- `من`;
- collective nouns;
- certain heritage patterns.

Examples such as plural reference after a collective may be valid when the intended meaning is a group. Do not erase such patterns automatically in classical or clearly heritage prose.

## 8. Verb before/after plural subject

In standard Arabic, when the verb precedes an overt plural subject, singular verb agreement is the ordinary pattern:

- `حضر الطلاب.`

When the plural human subject precedes the verb, full plural agreement is expected:

- `الطلاب حضروا.`

Classical Arabic records recognized non-majority patterns with plural/dual markers on a preverbal verb before an overt plural/dual subject. In heritage-sensitive proofreading, do not label every such attested pattern categorically ungrammatical without contextual reason.

## 9. Relative pronouns

Match the relative pronoun to the antecedent and to standard rational/non-rational agreement.

Examples:

- human masculine plural: `الطلاب الذين شاركوا`;
- human feminine plural: `الطالبات اللاتي شاركن`;
- non-human plural: `المبادرات التي نجحت`.

For non-human plurals, feminine singular reference is the normal standard pattern.

## 10. Followers: adjective, apposition, coordination, emphasis

A dependent follower normally tracks the syntactic case of the expression it follows when the construction requires concord.

Check especially:

- adjective (النعت);
- apposition/badal/`عطف البيان`;
- conjunction where both terms share the same role;
- emphasis.

Example:

- `قابلتُ الباحثةَ صاحبةَ المشروع.`

Do not change unrelated words in the phrase when one follower alone carries the defect.

## 11. `غير` and similar dependent expressions

`غير` is declinable and its case depends on its syntactic role. Do not freeze it into one case.

Examples:

- `جاء القوم غيرَ متأخرين.` — حال منصوب.
- `هذا رأيٌ غيرُ واضحٍ.` — adjective to a nominative noun.
- `مررت برأيٍ غيرِ واضحٍ.` — adjective to a genitive noun.

Determine whether `غير` is a state, adjective, exception noun, or another role before changing it.

## 12. Dual and sound masculine plural in idafa

When a dual or sound masculine plural is the first term of an idafa, its final nun is deleted.

Examples:

- `معلما المدرسة` / `معلمي المدرسة` according to case;
- `مهندسو المشروع` / `مهندسي المشروع` according to case.

Case still matters after nun deletion.

## 13. Five nouns

When the traditional conditions are met, the five nouns are inflected by letters:

- nominative: `أبو / أخو / حمو / فو / ذو`;
- accusative: `أبا / أخا / حما / فا / ذا`;
- genitive: `أبي / أخي / حمي / في / ذي`.

Example:

- `إن ذا الخبرة أقدر على التقدير.`

Do not apply this paradigm when the conditions are absent or the word is not being used in the relevant sense.

## 14. Visible case vs invisible case

Arabic proofreading must distinguish:

- **visible morphology**: `ان/ين`, `ون/ين`, deletion of nun, deletion of weak letter, five-noun forms, written hamza/alif changes;
- **optional case vowels**: final short vowels/tanwin usually omitted in ordinary prose.

Do not manufacture an error that exists only because an unvocalized word could theoretically take another case. Conversely, when the text explicitly includes a wrong case vowel or a visible case-bearing suffix, it is a real editable signal.

## 15. Minimality is not passivity

For proofreading:

- preserve every correct construction;
- but once a deterministic defect is established, correct it even if the sentence remains understandable;
- edit the smallest span that repairs the governing relation;
- do not rewrite surrounding style merely to make the correction look smoother.

A high-fidelity proofreader should be conservative about **style**, not conservative about **proven grammar errors**.

## 16. Ambiguity rule

If two grammatical readings remain genuinely plausible after checking the whole supplied context:

1. do not guess;
2. preserve the source in a pure proofreading task, unless the user asked to flag uncertainty;
3. if diagnostic output is requested, identify the competing analyses rather than presenting one as certain.

## Reference anchors

Use these as authoritative orientation when a rule needs deeper adjudication:

### Classical / heritage

- سيبويه — `الكتاب`.
- ابن هشام الأنصاري — `مغني اللبيب عن كتب الأعاريب`.
- ابن عقيل — `شرح ابن عقيل على ألفية ابن مالك`.

### Modern systematic grammars

- مصطفى الغلاييني — `جامع الدروس العربية`.
- عباس حسن — `النحو الوافي`.

When a modern prescriptive summary conflicts with a recognized classical licensed pattern, do not silently erase the older pattern in heritage-sensitive text. Route genuine variation to ambiguity/editorial judgment rather than deterministic correction.
